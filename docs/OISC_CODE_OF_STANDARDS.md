# OISC Code of Standards & Commissioner's Rules

This document provides the authoritative reference for the UK **Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner (OISC)** — now operating as the **Immigration Advice Authority (IAA)** — regulatory framework, covering the Code of Standards and the Commissioner's Rules.

---

## 1. Statutory Framework and Authority

### Primary Legislation
The regulatory regime governing immigration advice and services in the United Kingdom is established by **Part V of the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 (c. 33)**, as amended by subsequent legislation (including the Nationality, Immigration and Asylum Act 2002 and the Immigration Act 2014):

* **Section 82 (Interpretation):**
  * **"Immigration advice"** means advice which:
    * relates to a particular individual;
    * is given in connection with one or more relevant matters;
    * is given by a person who knows that he is giving it in relation to an individual or for the purpose of a particular case.
  * **"Immigration services"** means the making of representations on behalf of a particular individual:
    * in civil proceedings relating to a relevant matter before a court, tribunal or arbitrator; or
    * in formal correspondence with a minister or government department.
  * **"Relevant matters"** include:
    * claims for asylum or humanitarian protection;
    * applications for leave to enter or remain in the United Kingdom under the Immigration Rules;
    * unlawful entry, nationality, citizenship, and deportation;
    * bail applications and appeals before the First-tier Tribunal (IAC) or Upper Tribunal.

* **Section 84 (Provision of immigration services):**
  * Prohibits any person from providing immigration advice or immigration services unless they are a **qualified person**.
  * A person is qualified if they are:
    1. Registered with the Immigration Services Commissioner (OISC / IAA);
    2. Authorised by a designated qualifying regulator (e.g. Solicitors Regulation Authority, Bar Standards Board, CILEx Regulation, Law Society of Scotland);
    3. Working under the direct supervision of a qualified person.
  * **Section 91 (Offences):** Providing immigration advice or services in breach of Section 84 is a criminal offence punishable by fine and/or imprisonment.

* **Schedule 5 (The Commissioner):**
  * **Paragraph 1:** Grants statutory power to make **The Commissioner's Rules**.
  * **Paragraph 3(1):** Imposes a mandatory statutory duty on the Commissioner to prepare and issue a **Code of Standards** specifying the standards of conduct expected of registered persons and bodies.

## 3. The Regulatory Competence Framework (Levels 1–3)

The Commissioner regulates individual advisers and organisations across three defined competence levels and categories:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Level 3: Advocacy & Representation                          │
│  - First-tier & Upper Tribunal (IAC) advocacy               │
│  - Bail hearings and complex reconsideration                │
│  - Instructing counsel for Judicial Review                  │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Level 2: Casework & Complex Applications                    │
│  - Refusals, administrative reviews, human rights claims     │
│  - Complex asylum, detention, and deportation casework      │
│  - Discretionary leave applications outside Immigration Rules│
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Level 1: Initial Advice & Straightforward Applications       │
│  - Basic applications within Immigration Rules              │
│  - Standard work, visitor, student, and family routes       │
│  - Strictly non-complex, non-discretionary, non-appeals     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Level 1: Initial Advice
* Permitted only to handle straightforward applications satisfying standard Immigration Rules (e.g. standard visitor visas, straightforward student/graduate routes, standard British citizenship/naturalisation, basic spouse visa renewals).
* **Strict prohibition:** Cannot handle asylum claims, human rights claims, discretionary applications outside the rules, deportation matters, appeals, or judicial reviews.

### Level 2: Casework
* Permitted to handle complex applications, human rights applications outside the rules, initial asylum representations, applications involving overstayers, and administrative reviews.
* Can draft formal representations to UKVI / Home Office challenging adverse decisions.
* Cannot represent or advocate before the First-tier or Upper Tribunal.

### Level 3: Advocacy and Representation
* Permitted to conduct full appeals representation, file appeal notices, prepare bundles, and conduct oral advocacy before the First-tier Tribunal and Upper Tribunal (Immigration and Asylum Chamber).
* Permitted to lodge Judicial Review applications or instruct specialist barristers.

## 4. The Code of Standards: Core Principles

The Code of Standards sets out the mandatory professional conduct duties required of advisers and organisations:

### 1. Legality and Compliance (Standards 1–3)
* Advisers and organisations must act in accordance with UK law at all times.
* Advisers are authorised only to work for specific registered organisations and must operate strictly within their authorised Level and Categories.

### 2. Competence and Continuing Development (Standards 4–9)
* All organisations and advisers must remain fit and competent.
* Advisers must comply with mandatory Continuing Professional Development (CPD) requirements.
* Organisations must maintain written, Commissioner-approved supervision arrangements when any staff operate under supervision.
* No unauthorised person may provide immigration advice on behalf of the organisation.

### 3. Vulnerability and Client Care (Standards 10–13)
* **Vulnerability Protection:** Organisations and advisers must not take advantage of a client's or prospective client's vulnerability.
* **Non-Deception:** Advisers must not mislead clients or prospective clients.
* **Best Interests:** Advisers must act in their clients' best interests, subject to regulatory and legal requirements.
* **Document Authenticity:** Advisers must take all reasonably practicable steps to satisfy themselves that documents supplied in support of an application are genuine.

### 4. Integrity and Candour to Authorities (Standard 14)
* Advisers must show respect, politeness, and courtesy.
* Advisers must never mislead the Commissioner, UKVI, Home Office, or the courts/tribunals.
* Advisers must not knowingly or negligently permit themselves to be used in any deception.
* Advisers must not abuse UK immigration, asylum, or appellate procedures.

### 5. Equality and Diversity (Standards 15–17)
* Advisers must treat clients fairly without bias or discrimination, supported by a formal written Equality and Diversity policy.

### 6. Digital and Online Service Delivery (Standards 18–19)
* Organisations providing online immigration services must clearly state:
  * their authorised level and scope of services;
  * expected delivery timeframes and full fee transparency;
  * statutory consumer rights (including statutory cooling-off periods where applicable).

## 5. The Commissioner's Rules: Operational & Practice Rules

The Commissioner's Rules establish structural and governance safeguards for registered bodies:

1. **Fitness and Propriety:** All owners, directors, partners, and key personnel must satisfy background checks, bankruptcy restrictions, and honesty standards.
2. **Professional Indemnity Insurance (PII):** Mandatory cover tailored to turnover and caseload to ensure clients can recover damages in case of negligence.
3. **Client Accounts & Monies:** Clear separation between firm trading accounts and client accounts. Fees cannot be drawn until agreed milestones or casework stages are delivered.
4. **Record Keeping & Retention:** Detailed contemporaneous client records must be created and securely preserved for at least **6 years** following file closure.
5. **Complaints Procedure:** Every organisation must provide clients with a clear, accessible written complaints procedure detailing how to escalate issues directly to the Commissioner.

## 6. Platform Boundary Implications for LegalOS

LegalOS is architected to guarantee compliance with the OISC / IAA statutory framework:

1. **Not an AI Solicitor / Regulated Adviser:**
   * Under Section 84 of the Immigration and Asylum Act 1999, LegalOS does not provide individual legal advice or reserved legal representation.
   * Model outputs are structural navigations, rule checks, and fact extractions, and are never presented as licensed legal advice.

2. **Reserved Activity Guardrails:**
   * Workflows involving representations, formal submissions, and statutory appeals require qualified human sign-off.
   * As documented in `packages/repositories/src/review.ts` and `ReviewRepository.RV-G3`, reserved activities explicitly require a regulated human professional (OISC/IAA Level 2/3, SRA solicitor, or barrister) before execution.

3. **Audit Trails and Transparency:**
   * In line with OISC record-keeping principles, every execution, retrieved evidence snapshot, and decision gate in LegalOS is hash-linked and append-only, preserving an unalterable audit chain.
